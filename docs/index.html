Help & About

🌊 What is this?

This interactive website visualises the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) as a familiar attractor in phase space.

The phase diagram plots a climate variable T (sea surface temperature or sea level) against its rate of change dT/dt. The resulting trajectory — the ENSO attractor — reveals the low-dimensional, quasi-chaotic structure of El Niño dynamics.

Live data: NOAA CPC SST (1975–present) and UHSLC tide gauge sea level at Callao, Peru (1970–present).

Website: uferreira.github.io/el_nino
Source: github.com/uferreira/el_nino

📊 The Phase Diagram

The x-axis shows the filtered climate variable T; the y-axis shows its rate of change dT/dt (°C/month or mm/month).

The color gradient runs blue (earliest data) → red (most recent), so you can trace the direction of time.

Reading the diagram:

  • Right (T high): warm El Niño phase
  • Left (T low): cool La Niña phase
  • Top (dT/dt > 0): temperature rising — El Niño developing
  • Bottom (dT/dt < 0): temperature falling — El Niño decaying

The trajectory loops clockwise. Strong El Niño events (1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16) appear as large excursions to the right.

🔬 The Fourier Filter

A Fourier low-pass filter removes variability faster than ~9 months, isolating the ENSO signal (2–7 year periods).

  • HN1 = 10 months — pass edge: longer periods pass through
  • HN2 = 9 months — stop edge: shorter periods suppressed
  • NDOTS = 5 — sub-points per month for smoother curves

The filter is a faithful translation of a Fortran 77 reference program, verified to agree within 0.005°C. The derivative dT/dt is computed analytically from the Fourier representation — zero phase distortion, exact timing.

🎮 Duffing Equation Simulator

The simplest nonlinear oscillator that reproduces the ENSO attractor:

X'' = K1·X + K3·X³ + FA·cos(W·t + FF) + B·X'
  • K1 — linear restoring: positive → double-well potential
  • K3 — cubic restoring: negative → prevents divergence
  • FA — annual forcing amplitude (trade winds)
  • FF — forcing phase: timing of the annual forcing
  • B — damping: energy dissipation (must be negative)

Default values are fitted to match the observed SST attractor.

Steps/frame: integration steps per animation frame — higher = faster.
Frame delay: ms between frames — higher = slower, easier to watch.

🌀 Poincaré Section

Samples the phase diagram once per selected calendar month — one dot per year per dataset.

Blue = SST observed  ·  Green = Callao observed  ·  Red = Duffing model

Use the month dropdown to focus on a specific season. A periodic system would show 12 neat vertical columns. The scatter reveals the chaotic nature of ENSO: no two annual cycles are identical.

🔮 3D Familiar Attractor

The phase diagram extruded through time — the vertical axis is the calendar year. Each dot is one monthly data point, colored by calendar month (12 colors = Jan–Dec).

Click and drag to rotate the 3D view and reveal the fractal cross-section of the attractor. Vertical scatter within each month's column measures chaos directly.

The Duffing model (right) reproduces the general shape but not the exact trajectory — deterministic chaos amplifies tiny differences over time.
