Observed ENSO Attractor

What is a phase diagram? The x-axis shows the Fourier-filtered NINO1+2 sea surface temperature (SST, °C) and the y-axis shows its rate of change dT/dt (°C/month). Together they define a 2-dimensional phase space in which the ENSO cycle traces a quasi-periodic closed loop — the strange attractor. The colour gradient goes from blue (1975) to red (2026).

Reading the diagram: the trajectory moves clockwise. Starting at a cold La Niña state (lower-left), warming trade-winds push the system right (rising T), then upward (accelerating), until El Niño peaks in the upper-right. The return path (T falling, dT/dt negative) traces the lower arc back to La Niña. Major El Niño events (1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16) appear as large excursions. The loop never closes exactly — evidence of chaotic, low-dimensional ENSO dynamics.

Duffing Model Simulator

Why the Duffing oscillator? The observed ENSO attractor can be reproduced by a simple 2nd-order nonlinear ODE with a double-well potential V(X) = −K1X²/2 − K3X⁴/4 (K1 > 0, K3 < 0). The two wells correspond to El Niño (warm) and La Niña (cool) preferred states. Annual trade-wind forcing (FA, FF) drives the trajectory between wells. Adjust the sliders to explore how each parameter shapes the attractor. The RK4 integrator runs entirely in your browser.

Equation: X'' = K1·X + K3·X³ + FA·cos(W·t − FF°) + B·X'    W = 2π/12 rad/month
