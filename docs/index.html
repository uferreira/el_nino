Observed ENSO Attractor

What is a phase diagram? The x-axis shows the Fourier-filtered NINO1+2 sea surface temperature (SST, °C) and the y-axis shows its rate of change dT/dt (°C/month). Together they define a 2-D phase space in which the ENSO cycle traces a quasi-periodic closed loop — the strange attractor. Colour goes from blue (1975) to red (2026).

Reading the diagram: trajectory moves clockwise. La Niña starts lower-left, warming drives it right, El Niño peaks upper-right. Major events (1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16) show as large excursions. The loop never closes exactly — evidence of chaotic ENSO dynamics.

Callao Sea Level — Phase Diagram 1970–2026

Callao (12°S, 77°W) is a tide-gauge station on the coast of Peru, directly in the path of El Niño coastal warming. Sea level rises sharply during El Niño as downwelling Kelvin waves suppress coastal upwelling and warm water piles up against the South American coast. The phase diagram shows sea level anomaly (mm, mean removed) vs its rate of change — compare with the SST attractor above to see how both variables trace the same ENSO cycle with similar quasi-periodic structure.
Data: UHSLC station 093 (Callao, Peru) Period: Jan 1970 – Mar 2026 Filter: Fourier low-pass HN1=10 HN2=9 months NDOTS=5 Points: 3,361

Duffing Model Simulator

Why the Duffing oscillator? The double-well potential V(X) = −K1X²/2 − K3X⁴/4 (K1 > 0, K3 < 0) has two minima corresponding to warm (El Niño) and cool (La Niña) preferred states. Annual trade-wind forcing (FA, FF) drives the system between wells. Watch the attractor grow in real time — adjust Steps/frame and Frame delay to control animation speed.

What is the Poincaré section? The Poincaré section samples the phase diagram once per year in the selected calendar month — one dot per year per dataset. If ENSO were a simple periodic oscillator, all observed dots would land on the exact same point year after year. Instead, the dots scatter on a fractal set, revealing the chaotic, strange-attractor nature of ENSO dynamics. Comparing the observed scatter (blue=SST, green=Callao) with the Duffing model scatter (red) shows how well the double-well oscillator reproduces the fractal dimension and geometry of the real ENSO attractor.
