El Niño–Southern Oscillation

ENSO Dynamics · Phase-Space Analysis · Pacific Climate

Research

The El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is traditionally characterised using sea-surface temperature (SST) anomaly indices and atmospheric pressure variations. Here we examine ENSO from a dynamical-systems perspective by constructing phase-space trajectories from observed Niño 1+2 sea-surface temperatures and their temporal tendencies. Unlike conventional approaches that remove or suppress the seasonal cycle before analysis, we show that the annual cycle forms the fundamental orbital structure of the observed phase space. This orbit emerges from the seasonal alternation between enhanced coastal upwelling during austral winter and surface warming during austral summer in the eastern equatorial Pacific. El Niño and La Niña events appear as systematic deformations of this underlying orbit. Warm events are characterised by large excursions toward higher SSTs and substantially larger temperature tendencies than cold events, producing a pronounced asymmetry in phase-space geometry. The largest positive SST tendencies occur near January, while maximum SST anomalies are typically reached around March during El Niño years. These results indicate that Niño 1+2 provides a clear dynamical representation of observed ENSO evolution and suggest that eastern Pacific upwelling processes play a fundamental role in organising the geometry of the ENSO phase space.
