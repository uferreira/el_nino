Quasi-periodicity and Torus Diagnostics

Exploratory views of monthly NINO1+2, NINO3.4, and Callao records. Only samples at original monthly timestamps (irest = 0) are shown; Fourier sub-monthly interpolation points are excluded. NINO1+2 × Callao is the default because both describe the eastern Pacific; NINO3.4 is available as a basin-scale comparison.

How to read these plots. A thin, persistent closed curve in a yearly Poincaré section or a robust invariant surface in a delay reconstruction would be compatible with quasi-periodicity. A diffuse cloud may instead reflect noise, non-stationarity, missing variables, or high-dimensional dynamics. These filtered-data figures are diagnostics, not proof of either a KAM torus or chaos.

Help & About

🌊 What is this?

This interactive website visualises the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) as a familiar attractor in phase space.

The phase diagram plots a climate variable T (sea surface temperature or sea level) against its rate of change dT/dt. The resulting trajectory — the ENSO attractor — reveals the low-dimensional, quasi-chaotic structure of El Niño dynamics.

Live data: NOAA CPC SST (1975–present) and UHSLC tide gauge sea level at Callao, Peru (1970–present).

Website: uferreira.github.io/el_nino
Source: github.com/uferreira/el_nino

📊 The Phase Diagram

The x-axis shows the filtered climate variable T; the y-axis shows its rate of change dT/dt (°C/month or mm/month).

The historical trajectory uses a subdued color. The rolling latest 12 calendar months are always red, small points mark monthly filtered observations, and the larger gold point marks the current value.

Reading the diagram:

  • Right (T high): warm El Niño phase
  • Left (T low): cool La Niña phase
  • Top (dT/dt > 0): temperature rising — El Niño developing
  • Bottom (dT/dt < 0): temperature falling — El Niño decaying

The trajectory loops clockwise. Strong El Niño events (1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16) appear as large excursions to the right.

🔬 The Fourier Filter

A Fourier low-pass filter suppresses full periods below ~18 months, isolating interannual ENSO variability. HN1 and HN2 are half-period parameters.

  • HN1 = 10 months — half-period parameter: 20-month pass edge
  • HN2 = 9 months — half-period parameter: 18-month stop edge
  • NDOTS = 5 — sub-points per month for smoother curves

The filter reconstruction is verified against the Fortran 77 reference loops. The derivative dT/dt uses the corrected NT−1 monthly scale and is computed analytically from the Fourier representation — zero phase distortion.

🎮 Duffing Equation Simulator

The simplest nonlinear oscillator that reproduces the ENSO attractor:

X'' = K1·X + K3·X³ + FA·cos(W·t + FF) + B·X'
  • K1 — linear restoring: positive → double-well potential
  • K3 — cubic restoring: negative → prevents divergence
  • FA — annual forcing amplitude (trade winds)
  • FF — forcing phase: timing of the annual forcing
  • B — damping: energy dissipation (must be negative)

Default values are fitted to match the observed SST attractor.

Steps/frame: integration steps per animation frame — higher = faster.
Frame delay: ms between frames — higher = slower, easier to watch.

🌀 Poincaré Section

Samples the phase diagram once per selected calendar month — one dot per year per dataset.

Blue = SST observed  ·  Green = Callao observed  ·  Red = Duffing model

Use the month dropdown to focus on a specific season. A periodically locked system would concentrate samples at one or a few repeatable locations. Broad scatter alone does not prove chaos: it can also reflect stochastic forcing, non-stationarity, observational uncertainty, or unresolved variables.

🔮 3D Familiar Attractor

The phase diagram extruded through time — the vertical axis is the calendar year. Each dot is one monthly data point, colored by calendar month (12 colors = Jan–Dec).

Click and drag to rotate the 3D view and inspect the geometry from different angles. Vertical scatter may be consistent with irregular dynamics, but it does not measure chaos directly.

The Duffing model (right) reproduces the general shape but not the exact trajectory — deterministic chaos amplifies tiny differences over time.
